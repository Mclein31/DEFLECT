Pattern across cases: (a) orders placed in a third party's name/address (often a public figure), (b) COD payment mode, (c) the **rider** bears the upfront cash and the cancellation penalty, (d) the same fraudster places many orders in one day (14, 23). Pattern (d) is the exact signal the thesis targets (many accounts/orders linked by shared identity attributes).

## 2. Platform responses (PH)
- **Account bans on fake identities:** Grab PH said it suspended 351,000+ fake/incomplete/invalid accounts in 2019 and banned ~125,000 for frequent cancellation, multiple accounts, fraudulent GrabFood activity (per country head Brian Cu). [Inquirer, 11 Mar 2020](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1240010/grab-philippines-on-suspended-accounts/amp); [Coconuts](https://coconuts.co/manila/news/settle-down-back-there-grab-ph-warns-drunk-riders-suspends-351000-users-over-fake-names). Note: these figures are mainly ride-hailing/passenger accounts; do not present them as food-order fraud counts.
- **Account flags for no-shows/cancellers:** [ABS-CBN, 6 Jun 2019](https://www.abs-cbn.com/news/06/06/19/use-grabfood-responsibly-app-warns-of-penalties-to-those-who-cancel-orders).
- **Cash cap:** PHP 1,000 COD cap (above, Feb 2023). Current status in 2026 UNVERIFIED.
- **Rider reimbursement:** Grab reimbursed riders in the 2021 case; Grab said it was moving to a model where riders need not bring out cash. [Inquirer](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1297553/grab-ph-eyes-new-scheme-to-protect-food-couriers-vs-no-show-customers/amp) (date UNVERIFIED).
- **Foodpanda PH:** no PH-specific COD limit/blacklist statement found. A Singapore article reports COD limits and algorithmic blocking (attribution to foodpanda UNVERIFIED): [MustShareNews](https://mustsharenews.com/?p=337424).
- **Government:** DTI recommended online payment (Inquirer link above). CHR warned of legal action ([DZRH](https://dzrh.com.ph/post/chr-warns-legal-action-vs-fake-delivery-scammers), date UNVERIFIED); Sen. Hontiveros eyed legal action ([Inquirer](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1518776/fwd-hontiveros-eyes-possible-legal-action-vs-individuals-behind-fake-orders/)).

## 3. Legislation on "hoax ordering"
- **Senate Bill 2302 (Pimentel)**: passed Senate on 3rd reading **17 Jan 2022**, 23-0-0. Prohibits hoax orders (including ordering in another's name or under a fake name/address), cancelling confirmed orders, refusing unpaid orders; bars apps from making riders advance money; reports cite up to 6 months' jail. [Senate press release](https://legacy.senate.gov.ph/press_release/2022/0117_prib2.asp); [Manila Bulletin, 17 Jan 2022](https://mb.com.ph/2022/01/17/senate-approves-bill-protecting-delivery-riders-drivers-from-hoax-orders); [Rappler](https://www.rappler.com/nation/senate-approves-bill-jail-time-fake-delivery-orders/). (Congress number inferred from date; UNVERIFIED.)
- Earlier: SB 1677 (Lapid, 2021); Ejercito (2022) no-advance-payment bill. [PIA](https://mirror.pia.gov.ph/press-releases/2022/08/22/sen-bong-go-files-measure-to-protect-welfare-of-delivery-riders)
- 19th Congress: SB 1385 (Padilla, Oct 2022) [Senate](https://legacy.senate.gov.ph/press_release/2022/1014_padilla1.asp); HB 5467 "Delivery Riders Protection Act" (targets false-identity bogus orders) [HB05467](https://docs.congress.hrep.online/legisdocs/basic_19/HB05467.pdf); HB 8207 [HB08207](https://docs.congress.hrep.online/legisdocs/basic_19/HB08207.pdf); also [Inquirer: bill filed](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1658825/bill-seeking-to-protect-delivery-riders-from-bogus-buyers-filed-at-senate/amp) and [Inquirer: stricter measures urged](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1669593/stricter-measures-vs-fake-online-deliveries-urged) (dates UNVERIFIED).

- **Enactment status of any of these: UNVERIFIED.** No source found showing a law was passed. Check the Senate/House trackers (legacy.senate.gov.ph, congress.gov.ph) before claiming "no law exists". ## 4. COD share and fraud-loss statistics
- **COD share (PH e-commerce):** Xendit Ecommerce Payments Momentum Report 2026 (with Cube), released ~15 Oct 2026: COD ~**23%** of PH e-commerce **transaction value on Xendit's platform**, highest of six SEA markets (Vietnam ~16%); 1,000 PH-shopper survey; QR Ph + InstaPay up 198% Jan 2025-Apr 2026. Caveat: not national market volume; some outlets mislabel it as share of consumers. Sources: [Context.ph, 15 Oct 2026](https://context.ph/2026/10/15/cash-still-accounts-for-a-fourth-of-ph-e-commerce-sales/); [Daily Tribune, 20 Oct 2026](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/10/20/cash-on-delivery-still-dominates-phl-online-shopping); [GMA](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/money/companies/1006035/qr-ph-instapay-payments-nearly-triple-on-xendit-platform/story/); [e27, 15 Oct 2026](https://e27.co/cash-on-delivery-is-dying-in-southeast-asia-six-different-wallets-are-taking-its-place-20261015/).
- Other estimates: Worldpay via [Antom](https://www.antom.com/markets/philippines) 23% of 2025 e-commerce transactions; [2C2P](https://2c2p.com/articles/philippines-payment-methods/) citing IDC: ~22% in 2026, 15% by 2029; one report gives 11% (different method). Old "70-86% COD" claims (e.g., [Inquirer "7 in 10 Pinoys pay COD"](https://business.inquirer.net/?p=398779), undated) are stale/unsourced - avoid.
- **Food-delivery-specific COD share:** not found. UNVERIFIED.
- **Fraud losses:** no PH figure for fake-booking losses. Vendor/regional stats found: Incognia est. ~2% of monthly delivery-platform transactions in LATAM tied to coupon fraud (vendor research, LATAM - [Incognia guide](https://www.incognia.com/resources/essential-guide-to-promotion-abuse-on-food-delivery-platforms)); Incognia 2025 gig-economy report: 73% of respondents rank stopping multi-account creation as top priority ([BiometricUpdate, Oct 2025](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202510/multi-account-creation-biggest-challenge-in-gig-economy-incognia-fraud-report-finds)); Delivery Hero reported +32% blocked fraudulent purchases after graph approach ([AWS blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/database/empowering-fraud-detection-at-delivery-hero-with-amazon-neptune), date UNVERIFIED).

## 6. How to use this in the thesis
Problem statement can safely claim: (1) recurring, documented fake COD bookings in PH since 2020; (2) rider bears the cost; (3) repeated legislative attempts; (4) COD still ~1/5 of e-commerce value (2026). It must **not** claim a peso loss figure for platforms without a primary source. Frame the gap as: "no public quantification exists; this thesis uses synthetic data to evaluate detection."
